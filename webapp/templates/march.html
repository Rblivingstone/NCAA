Road to March

The rest of the {{ season_label }} season, simulated thousands of times from the current ratings. Each simulation plays every remaining game, every conference tournament, picks the NCAA field and plays the tournament, with the uncertainty in every team's rating carried through. Selection, seeding and the bracket follow the committee's own team-sheet numbers and bracketing rules. The odds below are the share of simulations in which each thing happened. They are refreshed every night.

How it works, and what it simplifies
  • Every game is a full simulated game from the model, the same one the rest of the site uses, with home court where it applies. Each simulation uses one sample of every team's skills, so a team the model is unsure about gets appropriately wide odds.
  • Conference tournaments: every member, seeded by simulated conference record, single elimination on a neutral floor, top seeds getting byes (the Ivy League takes its top four). Real formats vary a little.
  • Team sheets: in every simulation each team gets the numbers the committee reads. They're built from games between Division I teams only, as on the real sheets:
    • strength: the model's rating;
    • wins above bubble: each win or loss against the chance a bubble-quality team would have won that game, at that site;
    • a NET-style ranking: half strength, half results;
    • quadrant records by opponent ranking and site (Quadrant 1 is home against the top 30, neutral against the top 50, away against the top 75).
  • Selection: conference tournament champions get the automatic bids. The at-large places go down a committee score, the seed list follows the same score, and both line up with the committee's own sheets:
    • the score is mostly wins above bubble, then the NET-style ranking, then Quadrant 1 wins, minus Quadrant 3–4 losses;
    • on the real 2026 field it picks 35 of the 37 at-large teams, where strength and record alone picked 32.
  • The field: 76 teams from 2027, the expanded format. The Opening Round pairs the twelve lowest automatic bids and the twelve last at-large teams with their neighbours on the seed list.
  • The bracket follows the committee's published principles:
    • the top four seed lines go to the regional and first-round site nearest campus, in seed-list order, with the regions kept in balance;
    • a conference's top four teams on those lines go to different regions;
    • conference teams that met three times can't meet before the regional final, twice not before the regional semifinal, otherwise not in the first round;
    • no team plays at an arena where it played more than three regular-season games;
    • BYU only goes to Thursday–Saturday sites;
    • a team moves one seed line when nothing else satisfies the rules (in the few simulations where even that fails, the fewest rules are broken);
    • the Final Four pairs the regions of the No. 1 seeds 1 v 4 and 2 v 3.
    Placing the real 2026 seed list this way puts 12 of the 16 top-four seeds in their actual region and 13 at their actual first-round site.
