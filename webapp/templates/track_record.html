Track record: ten tournaments, out of sample

For each of the last ten NCAA tournaments, the model was fitted only on that season's games before the First Four, as it stood on Selection Sunday, and asked to forecast the whole bracket. Nothing from the tournament, and nothing from later seasons, went into it. Here it is head to head with the alternatives whose historical forecasts can be recovered.

Game forecasts
Each source's chance that the eventual winner would win that game. Lower log loss and Brier score are better. Every row is compared with the model on exactly the games that source covers. ESPN BPI and Vegas are set on the day of each game, after the earlier rounds are known, so they have information the Selection Sunday forecasts don't.
Brackets at Selection Sunday
Each source's most likely bracket, found by the same exact search, scored with ESPN Tournament Challenge points (10-20-40-80-160-320, 1,920 at most). "Expected" is the score the source itself expected of that bracket. The five-bracket portfolio is the most likely bracket for each of the source's five likeliest champions, scored by the best of the five. ESPN BPI and Vegas only forecast single games, so they have no Selection Sunday bracket.

How the test works, and what's missing
  • The model: refitted from scratch for each season, the same way the live site fits, on regular-season and conference-tournament games only. Its game chances are its Selection Sunday chances for each pairing, never updated during the tournament. It was designed with knowledge of recent seasons, so this is out of sample for the fitted numbers, not for every modelling choice.
  • Seeds: a curve in the seed difference fitted on the tournaments before each year (from 2012).
  • Efficiency margin: a KenPom-style rating (opponent- and venue-adjusted points per 100 possessions) rebuilt here from the same box scores the model sees. It is not KenPom's own numbers.
  • ESPN BPI and Vegas (the consensus line, moneylines with the bookmaker's margin removed): read from ESPN's archived game files, which keep them for some years only (the Years column). Each is compared with the model on the games it covers.
  • Not included, because their historical forecasts can't be recovered:
    • KenPom itself: its archive is behind a subscription;
    • FiveThirtyEight: its public archive ends in 2015, and it stopped forecasting after 2023;
    • BracketProof: no record of its forecasts could be found.
  • 2020 had no tournament. In 2021, VCU–Oregon was declared a no-contest: Oregon advanced for bracket scoring, but no game is counted for forecast accuracy.
